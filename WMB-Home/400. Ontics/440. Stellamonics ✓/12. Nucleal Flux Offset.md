# Flus Offset Factor (FOF)

The Flux Offset Factor (FOF) is a dimensionless measure of stellar irradiance at a given orbit relative to the nucleal orbit, expressed as a signed offset. The nucleal orbit is defined as the reference orbit (FOF = 0), corresponding to the stellar irradiance received by Earth around the Sun.

Positive values of FOF indicate _intranucleal_ excess stellar flux, while negative values indicate _extranucleal_ flux deficit. FOF does not itself describe habitability; rather, it provides a physically grounded coordinate from which habitability-related interpretations may be derived.

From a human-centered perspective, an Earth-like planet at or near the nucleal orbit has the highest likelihood of Earth-like surface conditions, all else being equal.

$$
\begin{align}
FOF &= \frac{N}{D^2} - 1 \\[1ex]
\text{But, since N} &:= \text{1 in this instance, the equation simplifies to:} \\[1ex]
FOF &= \frac{1}{D^2} - 1 
\end{align}
$$

Where:
- $FOF$ = the numeric value of the Flux Offset Factor
- *$D$* = the orbit's distance in AU (relative to the nucleal orbit)

Values of $FOF > 0$ indicate that the orbit is _intranucleal_ and values of $FOF < 0$ indicate that the orbit is extranucleal.  The total range of $FOF \in (-1, \infty)$, but for our purposes the limiting range is $FOF \in (-0.96, 3.0)$, as tabulated below

Values of $FOF>0$ indicate that an orbit is _intranucleal_, while values of $FOF<0$ indicate that it is _extranucleal_. In principle, the Flux Offset Factor spans the range $FOF \in (−1,\infty)$, approaching $−1$ asymptotically with increasing orbital distance and diverging without bound as distance decreases.

For practical purposes within Wimby, analysis is typically restricted to the interval $FOF \in (−0.96, 3.0)$, corresponding to the conventional outer and inner orbital bounds tabulated below.

## Normalized Equilibrium Temperature

While the Flux Offset Factor (FOF) describes how stellar irradiance varies with orbital distance, **temperature responds to irradiance nonlinearly**.  As a result, temperature varies much more gradually than flux across orbital space.

$$
\begin{align}
NET &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{D}} \\[1ex]

\end{align}
$$

Where:
- $NET$ = the numeric value of the orbit's Normalized Equilibrium Temperature
- *$D$* = the orbit's distance in AU (relative to the nucleal orbit)

![[FOF Temperature Graph.png]]

**Normalized equilibrium temperature as a function of normalized orbital distance.**  
The curve shows equilibrium temperature $T/T_N$​ derived from stellar irradiance via Stefan–Boltzmann scaling, assuming an airless body in radiative equilibrium. Temperature changes more gradually than flux, rising rapidly at small orbital distances and declining slowly with increasing distance. The curve passes through the nucleal orbit at $(\dfrac{D}{N} = 1,\; \dfrac{T}{T_N} = 1)$ and approaches zero asymptotically at large distances.

FOF:Two features of this curve are especially important:

1. **Thermal buffering relative to flux.**  
    Although stellar flux increases or decreases rapidly with orbital distance, temperature responds more slowly. Large changes in irradiance correspond to comparatively modest changes in equilibrium temperature. This buffering effect explains why atmospheres, albedo, and heat transport can substantially modify surface conditions without altering the underlying stellar energy budget.
    
2. **Asymmetric thermal response.**  
    As with flux, the temperature curve is asymmetric about the nucleal orbit. Inward motion produces relatively rapid heating, while outward motion results in a long, shallow decline. Even at large distances, equilibrium temperature approaches zero only asymptotically, never reaching it.

Like FOF, NET is **purely physical**. It does not encode assumptions about atmospheres, phase states of water, biology, or habitability. Instead, it provides a second, derived coordinate that translates stellar energy availability into thermal terms. Thermozones, animozones, and anthropic interpretations are applied subsequently as overlays, not embedded in the temperature function itself.

Together, FOF and NET form a complementary pair: FOF describes how much energy is available, while $T/T_N$​ shows how that energy expresses itself thermally.
