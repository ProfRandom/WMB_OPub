## A Note on Concepts, Terms, and Language

Wimby does **not** begin with a fixed vocabulary.

It begins with **questions**.

As the framework developed, it became clear that many familiar terms — especially those borrowed from astronomy, astrobiology, and worldbuilding lore — were doing too much work, or the wrong kind of work. Some were overloaded. Others were vague. Many quietly collapsed distinct ideas into a single word.

Rather than force precision into inherited language, Wimby allows **language to grow where precision is required**.

As a result, you will encounter new terms here.

They are not invented for novelty.  
They exist because something _already existed_ — conceptually, structurally, physically — and did not yet have a clean, non-misleading name.

This is not a lexicon-driven system.  
Terms are introduced **only where they earn their keep**, and always in context.

You are not expected to memorize them.  
You are expected to _recognize what they are pointing at_.

If a concept feels intuitive once named, the name has done its job.  
If a familiar word would have blurred the distinction, it was not used.

Wimby makes no claim that its terminology is final, universal, or privileged.

It claims only this:

> **Clarity sometimes requires saying, “we need a better word for this.”**

And then using it — plainly, consistently, and without apology.
### What This Means for You

- You do **not** need to learn Wimby’s vocabulary before engaging with it    
- You do **not** need to agree with every term to use the framework    
- You are free to adapt, rename, or replace language in your own work    

What matters is not the words themselves, but the **distinctions they preserve**.

Wimby is concerned with _seeing clearly_, not _naming authoritatively_.

If the concepts hold, the language can evolve.  
If the concepts fail, no terminology will save them.
