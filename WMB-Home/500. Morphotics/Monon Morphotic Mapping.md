# Monon Morphotic Mapping
**Monon Morphotic Mapping** describes *where* morphotypes become meaningful across the mass–complexity continuum of monons. It does **not** define the morphotypes themselves—those live in **Duramon Morphotics**. Instead, this article maps the transitions between monon classes and identifies the regimes in which Lithiform, Rheiform, Aeriform, and Ulsiform structures can arise.
## Overview
Morphotics becomes relevant once a monon has enough mass to differentiate, sustain structural phases, and express coherent geochemical or fluid regimes. This begins with the **Varymons** and becomes fully expressed in the **Duramons**.

## The Varymon Mass Regimes

Morphotic expression maps onto the **Duramon** class and its internal mass subclasses. The Varymon Series is not a linear evolutionary ladder; it is a set of parallel mass regimes across different monon superclasses.

### **Monon Superclasses**
- **Synamons** — cohesion without gravitation (pre-duramonic)
- **Duramons** — gravitationally-coherent bodies with solid/liquid phases  
- **Transamons** — partially degenerate bodies (brown-dwarf class)
- **Fusamons** — self-fusing stellar bodies  
- **Peramons** — supermassive, collapse-dominated structures  

### **Duramon Subclasses (Mass-Based)**
Within the *Duramon* superclass, morphotics expresses itself through the following mass regimes:

- **Micramons** — 10⁻⁶–10⁻⁵ t  
  Dust-to-boulder scale; no stable differentiation

- **Minimons** — 10⁻⁴ t  
  Small minor bodies; weak early morphotics

- **Midimons** — 10⁻³ t  
  Major minor bodies; first reliable differentiation

- **Mesamons** — 10⁻² t  
  Dwarf-planemons; stable lithic crusts and internal layering

- **Planemons** — 10⁻¹–10¹ t  
  Full planetary bodies; complete morphotic maturation

Morphotics applies **only** within the Duramon family.  
Transamons, Fusamons, and Peramons express **ulsiform** regimes instead, not lithiform/rheiform/aeriform morphotypes.

For morphotype definitions, see **Duramon Morphotics**.


Morphotics begins at **Micramons**, matures at **Midimons**, and becomes fully stable at the **Planemon** scale.
## Where Each Morphotype Appears

*(See [[Duramon Morphotics]] for full definitions of each morphoform.)*
### Lithiforms
- Require density and internal pressure sufficient for mineral differentiation  
- First appear in mid-sized **Minimons**  
- Fully realized in **Mesamons** and **Planemons**
### Rheiforms
- Require partial melting or cryomelt regimes  
- Common in **icy Mesamons** and **outer-system Planemons**
### Aeriforms
- Require atmospheric mass retention  
- Appear at mid-Mesamon scale  
- Fully expressed in **Planemons** with sustained atmospheres
### Ulsiforms
- Require degenerate or exotic internal pressures  
- Only appear in **Transamons**, **Fusamons**, and **Peramons**
## Morphotic Expression by Monon Class

### Micramons → Minimons
- Weak morphotic expression  
- Insufficient differentiation for stable lithiformity  
- Cryo-rheiform behaviors common in outer-regime bodies
### Midimons
- First stable lithiform crusts and differentiated interiors  
- Rheiform structures appear in volatile-rich bodies  
- Can hold thin, transient aeriform envelopes
### Mesamons
- Fully differentiated interiors  
- Reliable lithiformity  
- Early tectonic regimes
### Planemons
- Full morphotic maturity  
- All four morphoforms (Lithiform, Rheiform, Aeriform, Ulsiform) definable by composition, structure, and internal energetics  
- This is the realm of **Duramon Morphotics**
