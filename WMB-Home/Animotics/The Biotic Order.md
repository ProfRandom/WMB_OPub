# What the Biotic Order Is

The **Biotic Order** defines the region of possibility in which **carbon–oxygen–hydrogen (COH) chemistry** can sustain organized, persistent, energy-cycling systems recognizable as _animosys_ ("life").

It does **not** assert that animosys exists.  
It asserts that animosys is **physically possible**.

A world classified as Biotic possesses the minimum structural and environmental coherence required for:

- liquid-phase COH chemistry    
- accessible energy gradients    
- stable molecular complexity    
- long-lived metabolic cycling    

Everything else in animotics builds on this threshold.
## Axis Placement (Explicit and Non-Negotiable)

The Biotic Order is defined **exclusively** on the **[[Animotics Overview|Amegic Axis]]**.

- **Amegic Axis:** COH-amegic    
- **Anthropic Axis:** _not applied at this level_    
- **Morphotic Axis:** _not specified_    

This matters.

The Biotic Order does **not** describe:

- how comfortable a world is for humans    
- what kind of body hosts the environment    
- whether animosys actually emerges    

Those are downstream questions.
## What “Biotic” Means (and What It Doesn’t)

**Biotic means “chemically eligible for COH-animosys.”**

It does _not_ mean:

- Earthlike    
- breathable    
- temperate    
- inhabited    
- benign    

A Biotic world may be:

- lethal to humans    
- sterile due to history    
- climatically unstable    
- chemically hostile at the surface    

Yet still remain Biotic if its underlying physical regime permits COH metabolism _in principle_.

> **Biotic is a structural permission, not a biological promise.**
## The Role of Structure vs. History

The Biotic Order concerns **structure**, not outcome.

Whether animosys actually appears depends on **milieutics**:

- stellar radiation history    
- volatile delivery    
- atmospheric evolution    
- catastrophic resets    
- chemical path dependence    

Two physically indistinguishable planemons may diverge completely:

- one teeming with animosys    
- one forever sterile    

Both can still be Biotic.  This separation is intentional.  It prevents animotics from collapsing into astrobiological hindsight.
## Relation to Other Animotic Classes

The Biotic Order is the **parent class** for all COH-based animosys.

From it emerge several **anthropic strata**, which classify _human compatibility_, not substrate:

- **Terric Domain** — mesanthropic  
    COH-compatible worlds survivable by humans with technology.
    
- **Geotic Realm** — maxanthropic  
    Broadly Earthlike worlds with stable, moderate environments.
    
- **Gaean Province** — melanthropic  
    Worlds that fall into the deep anthropic sweet spot: “shirtsleeve planets.”    

In parallel—not nested—exists:

- **Kybelic Domain** — melamegic  
    COH-worlds optimized for biospheric productivity rather than human comfort.    

All of these are **Biotic**.  
None redefine what Biotic means.
## Biotic vs. Xenotic (Critical Boundary)

The Biotic Order is **chemically specific**.  It applies exclusively to:

- carbon backbones    
- oxygen-mediated energetics    
- hydrogen solvents    

Worlds whose animosys depends on fundamentally different substrates—ammonia, methane, silicates, plasmas, fields—are **not Biotic**, regardless of how “alive” they may be.

Those belong to the [[Xenotic Order (old)|**Xenotic Order**]] or **[[Astatic Order|Astatic Order]]**.

A world may be:

- physically Earth-sized    
- gravity-normal    
- atmospherically thick    

…and still **not Biotic** if its chemistry lies outside COH viability.

Substrate, not shape, draws the line.
## Time Matters: Biotic Is a State, Not a Trait

Animotic classification is **state-based**.

A single world may pass through multiple animotic states across its history:

- pre-Biotic → Biotic    
- Biotic → Terric    
- Terric → Geotic    
- Geotic → Kybelic (temporarily)    
- Biotic → non-Biotic (via loss of solvent or chemistry)    

Biotic is therefore not a permanent label.  
It is a **window of possibility** that can open and close.
## Why the Biotic Order Exists in Wimby

Worldbuilders need a way to say:

> “This planet _could_ host Earth-type animosys, even if it doesn’t.”

The Biotic Order provides that answer — cleanly, conservatively, and without narrative bias.

It is the **floor** of COH animosys, not the ceiling.

Everything above it adds specificity.  
Everything below it removes the possibility entirely.
## Relationship to Planemonics (Explicit but Secondary)

The Biotic Order is **not defined by mass, radius, gravity, or density**.  However:

- certain planemonic regimes **correlate strongly** with Biotic eligibility    
- others **exclude it outright**    

These correlations are discussed **elsewhere**, where they belong: in articles concerned with **design guidance**, not ontology.

> Biotic defines _what kind of animosys is possible_.  
> Planemonics constrains _which bodies can host it_.

The arrow only points one way.
## Summary (for Fast Readers)

- **Biotic = COH-animosys is physically possible**    
- It is an **amegic classification**, not an anthropic one    
- It says nothing about comfort, habitability, or inhabitants    
- It is the parent class of Terric, Geotic, Gaean, and Kybelic worlds    
- It is distinct from Xenotic and Astatic animosys    
- It is state-based, historically contingent, and deliberately conservative    

If a world is not Biotic, **COH-animosys cannot arise there at all**.  
If it _is_ Biotic, everything else is still up for grabs.
