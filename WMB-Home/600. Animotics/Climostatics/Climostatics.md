# WMB Global Biomic Lexicon Ordered Cold → Hot (Latitudinal Gradient)

## ❄️ COLD / HIGH-LATITUDE ZONE
1. **Polaric** — Ice sheet / polar cap    
2. **Tundric** — Tundra    
3. **Alpinic** — Alpine (cold, high-elevation analog to tundra)    
4. **Siberic** — Frozen/periglacial grassland    
5. **Boreic** — Boreal forest (cold coniferous band)    
6. **Gobic** — Cold desert (arid, high-latitude or continental)   
## 🌤️ COOL–TEMPERATE ZONE
7. **Steppic** — Cold-temperate grassland (steppe)    
8. **Prairic** — Warm-temperate grassland    
9. **Austeric** — Temperate deciduous/mixed forest    
10. **Cascadic** — Temperate rainforest    
11. **Chaparric** — Mediterranean shrubland / dry scrub
    
## ☀️ WARM–TROPICAL ZONE
12. **Veldtic** — Warm, subtropical grassland    
13. **Serengetic** — Hot, dry tropical grassland/shrubland    
14. **Savannic** — Hot, moist tropical grassland (true savanna)    
15. **Sundalic** — Tropical deciduous / monsoon forest    
16. **Saharic** — Hot desert    
17. **Amazonic** — Tropical rainforest (hottest + wettest on Earth analogs)

>Note: Higher _altitudes_ in lower _latitudes_ are equivalent to higher _latitude_ biomes:
### Cold-to-Hot *Latitudinal* Ladder (horizontal)

Polaric → Tundric → Alpinic → Siberic → Boreic → Steppic → Prairic → Austeric → Chaparric → Veldtic → Serengetic → Savannic → Sundalic → Saharic → Amazonic
### Hot-to-Cold *Altitudinal* Ladder (vertical, inverted)

Amazonic → Sundalic → Savannic → Serengetic → Veldtic → Chaparric → Austeric → Prairic → Steppic → Boreic → Siberic → Alpinic → Tundric → Polaric
- Note that the aridic (desert) biomes don't occur in this sequence.

# Summary

**Climostatics** and **[[Atmospherics]]** together provide a complete descriptive framework for understanding a planet’s environmental character.
- **Climostatics** identifies the _global biome_ that dominates the planet’s surface—its broad temperature–moisture identity.  
    _Polaric, Boreic, Steppic, Prairic, Chaparric, Savannic, Saharic, Amazonic,_ and so on.    
- **Atmospherics** describes the _dynamic behavior_ of the atmosphere—its temperament.  
    _Tempestal, Zephyral,_ or _Serenal._
    
These two layers operate independently but combine naturally to express a world’s overall feel.  
- A planet may be _Saharic–Tempestal_, _Prairic–Zephyral_, _Amazonic–Serenal_, or any other pairing.  
- Writers and worldbuilders can use these combinations to quickly communicate both the **surface environment** and the **weather personality** of a world without technical jargon.

Together, Climostatics and Atmospherics transform planetary description from a vague label (“desert planet”) into a clear, elegant environmental profile—one that is easy to imagine, easy to compare, and easy to build from.
