# The Ontic Hierarchy: Ontic Regimes

**Ontic Regimes** describe the dominant cohesive forces that define how matter and energy maintain unity.  
> They trace the progression from the smallest bound structures to the largest self-coherent bodies — **from nuclei to stars and beyond.**

| **Regime**                               | **Dominant Binding Forces**              | **Scale**                                           | **Examples**                             |
| :--------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------- |
| Nuclear Regime                           | Strong and Weak Nuclear                  | Subatomic and Nuclear                               | neutronium, fusion cores                 |
| **Microphysical Regime**                 | Electroweak<br>(atomic/molecular EM)<br> | Atomics, Ions, <br>Molecular and Crystalline matter | atoms, elements, compounds               |
| **Macrophysical Regime**<br>(*Mononics*) | Electrochemical and Gravitic             | Planetary, degenerate, and stellar bodies           | duromons, mesomons, fusomons, peromons   |
| **Field Regime**                         | Energetic or spacetime curvature fields  | Exotic, collapse-bound, or post-stellar systems     | black holes, cryptomons, hyperstellamons |

Each regime represents a distinct **mode of coherence**, determined by the dominant physical interaction at its scale.  
Together they form the backbone of **Ontic hierarchy**, linking the smallest bound particle to the largest self-coherent body.

## From Ontic Regimes to Mononics

For WMB purposes, the **Macrophysical Regime** is where our real work begins.  
The Nuclear, Microphysical, and Field regimes matter — they set the stage, establish constraints, and occasionally impress themselves upon the macrocosm — but they are not where **worldcrafters** operate day-to-day.

> **Mononics** is the domain of worldcrafters:  
> the study of _self-coherent bodies_ — moons, planets, mesomons, stellamons, and all other macro-objects whose behavior is shaped by bulk cohesion or gravity.

This is the level at which crafted worlds acquire their physical identity and begin expressing the physical characteristics that will later shape and support climates, life, culture, and story.

# Ontic Mass Continuum
## Introduction

**Mass intervals describe scale, but not class.**  
A monon’s [[02. The Ontic Mass Continuum|_Ontic Class_]] (duromon, mesomon, fusomon, peromon) depends on its **internal physics** — whether cohesion is electrochemical, gravitational, degenerate-electron, or fusional — not solely the amount of mass it contains.

Mass domains are therefore **diagnostic**, not **determinative**:  
they tell you _how big something is_, not _what kind of object it is_.

The following conventions establish how **Worldmaking Basics (WMB)** names and interprets mass intervals relative to Earth’s mass ($t$). These definitions ensure that both scientific notation and symbolic terminology remain intuitive across a vast range of scales.

- Where official SI prefixes exist (e.g., *micro-*, *milli-*, etc.), **WMB** uses them directly.
- For intermediate powers without SI prefixes, **WMB** adopts consistent [[Wimby Extension of the SI Prefix System|neologisms]] based on the exponent’s magnitude:
	- Exponents **below zero** use an *-i* ending (unless an SI prefix already exists, e.g., *micro-*, not *micri-*).  
	-  Exponents **above zero** use an *-a* ending (unless an SI prefix already exists, e.g., *kilo-*, not *kila-*).
- While SI defines prefixes beyond *exa-* ([Metric prefix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_prefix)), **WMB** rarely uses them in Ontic contexts.  
   - Even the observable universe spans only ≈ $46.5$ **giga**light-years (Gly) in radius — roughly three orders of magnitude below **teraterran** relevance.  
- The **negative intervals** are symbolically essential: many moons, asteroids, and micro-monons fall far below $1t$ in mass.  
   - It is often **clearer** to say  
	   - “The mass is $1$ microterran $(μt)$”  than  “The body is $0.000001t$.”  
- For practical purposes, **WMB** defines the upper bound of each mass interval as **one demiterran** ($10^{-5};\;0.00001t$) below the next power of ten.  
   - Thus, the *terran* mass interval **technically spans** 
     $1.0\,t ≤ M < 9.99999\,t$.  
   - Any value exceeding that threshold — even slightly — **rounds up** to the next symbolic interval.  
     - **Example:**
        $9.999 99 1\,t → 10.0\.t$, assigning the body to the **dekaterran** interval.  
- The **deniterran** marks the lower limit of the scale; bodies smaller than this cannot achieve hydrostatic equilibrium.  
- The **exaterran** marks the upper limit, approximating the realistic mass ceiling for Ultra-massive Black Holes (UMBHs), about $10^{12}\,M\,s$ ([Supermassive_black_hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermassive_black_hole#Description)).
## The Microterran Subscale

Because many **monons** have masses best expressed as a **millionth of a terran**, WMB defines an optional *microterran subscale* ($μt$) for intuitive symbolic modeling.

This subscale maps cleanly to the standard symbolic intervals and absolute powers of ten.

$$
\begin{array}{l r r l r r r}
\textbf{$\mu$terran Scale} &
\textbf{Min. Mass} &
\textbf{Max. Mass} &
\textbf{Std. Scale} &
\textbf{Power} \\[3pt]
\hline\hline
\text{demimicro (dmμt)}  & 0.0001 & 0.001  & \text{deniterran}  & 10^{-10} \\
\text{millimicro (mμt)}  & 0.001  & 0.01   & \text{nanoterran}  & 10^{-9}  \\
\text{centimicro (cμt)}  & 0.01   & 0.1    & \text{oktiterran}  & 10^{-8}  \\
\text{decimicro (dμt)}   & 0.1    & 1      & \text{septiterran} & 10^{-7}  \\
\text{microterran (μt)}  & 1      & 10     & \text{microterran} & 10^{-6}  \\
\text{dekamicro (daμt)}  & 10     & 100    & \text{pentiterran} & 10^{-5}  \\
\text{hectomicro (hμt)}  & 100    & 1000   & \text{demiterran}  & 10^{-4}  \\
\text{kilomicro (kμt)}   & 1000   & 10000  & \text{milliterran} & 10^{-3}  \\
\text{myriamicro (Mrμt)} & 10000  & 100000 & \text{centiterran} & 10^{-2}  \\
\text{quintamicro (Qnμt)}& 100000 & 1000000& \text{deciterran}  & 10^{-1}  \\
\end{array}
$$

The microterran subscale preserves the same logarithmic spacing as the full terran sequence but simplifies relative comparisons for sub-planetary bodies — e.g., moons, asteroids, or micro-monons — within a single stellar system.

It improves readability and narrative clarity for small-mass bodies such as **Vesta**, **Miranda**, and **Ceres**.

- It is especially useful for monons with masses between $10^{-6}\,t$ and $10^{-1}\,t$, where decimal terran mass values become visually dense or cognitively opaque.  
- This symbolic shorthand allows you to say:
	- “Ceres is about $157$ **microterrans** ($157\,μt$)”
		- instead of “Ceres is $0.000157\,t$.”  

> **In short:** The $μt$ unit supports clarity without sacrificing scalar precision — a vital aid to both symbolic modeling storytelling.  While most useful in describing small-mass duromons, these subscale prefixes can be used wherever a scaling prefix is needed.

## Applications and Examples

$$
\begin{array}{lrll}
\textbf{Example Monon} & \textbf{Mass ($t$)} & \textbf{Symbolic} & \text{Domain}\\
\hline\hline
\text{Miranda} & 0.0000076 & 7.60\text{ μt} & \text{microterran}\\
\text{Ceres} & 0.000157 & 1.57 \text{\;dmt} & \text{demiterran}\\
\text{Luna} & 0.0123 & 1.23 \text{\;ct} & \text{centiterran}\\
\text{Proxima b} & 1.27	& 1.27\text{ t} & \text{terran}\\
\text{Jupiter} & 317.8 & 3.18\text{\;Ht} & \text{hectoterran}
\end{array}
$$


These examples illustrate how WMB notation maintains both intuitive scale and quantitative accuracy across ten orders of magnitude.

Note that the Ontic mass intervals are *descriptive*, not definitive, in WMB: they are part of what describes an object, but they do not define the classification of that object.  What makes fusomons different from duromons is more than their larger mass interval.
