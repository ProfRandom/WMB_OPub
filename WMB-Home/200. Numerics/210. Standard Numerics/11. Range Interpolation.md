 # Range Interpolation
 Sometimes it is necessary to find a value that lies "somewhere between two values" — and sometimes you want to translate that proportion between two different ranges.  This article explains how to do that.

## Finding a Percentage

Calculating percentages is a well-known procedure.  Divide the number you have by the maximum number and multiply the quotient by 100:

$$
\begin{aligned}
pct &= \frac{x}{max} · 100 
\end{aligned}\tag{1}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Examples:} \\
(1)\quad pct &= \frac{3.5}{10} \cdot 100 = 0.35 \cdot 100 = 35(\%) \\ 
(2)\quad pct &= \frac{4.6}{8} \cdot 100 = 0.575 \cdot 100 = 57.5(\%)
\end{aligned}
$$

Both examples assume a range that begins at zero and extends to a maximum value.

### Percentage of A Known Range

But what if your sample runs from $(6 ··\;21)$ and you need to know what percentage of that range $13.2$ represents?  You can do this.

The equation is very similar:

$$
\begin{aligned}
pct &= \frac{x - min}{max - min} · 100 \\
\end{aligned}\tag{2}
$$

Where:
- $x$ = the number you are checking ($13.2$ in our example)
- $min$ = the smallest number in your range ($6$ in our example)
- $max$ = the largest number in your range ($21$ in our example)

$$
\begin{aligned}
pct &= \frac{13.2 - 6}{21 - 6} · 100 \\[1ex]
&= \frac{7.2}{15} \cdot 100 \\[1ex]
&= 0.48 \cdot 100 \\[1ex]
pct &= 48(\%)
\end{aligned}
$$

$13.2$ is $48$ percent of the range from $6$ to $21$.

## Calculating a Percent Value

It is also well-known how to figure out what number is a given percentage of another number.  For instance, what is $37$% of $66$?:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= \frac{pct}{100} \cdot max 
\end{aligned}\tag{3}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Example: } x &= \frac{37}{100} \cdot 66 \\[1ex]
&= 0.37 \cdot 66 \\[1ex]
x &= 24.42
\end{aligned}
$$

Now we know that $37$% of $66$ is $24.42$.

### Percentage Value of A Range

Now, though, what if you want to know what value is represented by $17$% of the range between $(11··\;52)$?

Just as we rearranged the first equation above to give us the second:

$$
\begin{aligned}
pct = \frac{x}{max} \cdot 100 \quad \longrightarrow \quad x = \frac{pct}{100} \cdot max
\end{aligned}
$$

We can rearrange:

$$
\begin{aligned}
pct &= \frac{x - min}{max - min} · 100 \\
\end{aligned}\tag{2}
$$
$$
\begin{array}{l l}
\end{array}
$$
to:
$$
\begin{array}{l l}
x &= \left[\dfrac{pct}{100} \cdot (max - min)\right] + min
\end{array}\tag{4}
$$

And now we can calculate what number represents 42% of the range from $(17 ··\;49)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x & = \left[\dfrac{42}{100} \cdot (49 - 17)\right] + 17 \\[1.5ex]
& = \left[0.49 \cdot 32\right] + 17 \\[1.5ex]
& = 15.68 + 17 \\[1.5ex]
x & = 32.68
\end{aligned}
$$
 
 — and we now know that $32.68$ is $42$% of the range from $(17 ··\;49)$.

## Transferring A Percentage Between Ranges

This is just a matter of combining Eq. 2 and Eq. 4, above.

Let's say we have a range $(4817 ··\;5349)$ and a value $x = 5118$.  We want to know what percentage of the range $5118$ represents, and then transfer that percentage to another range $(6233 ··\;7165)$.

Find the percentage of the first range:

$$
\begin{aligned}
pct &= \frac{5118 - 4817}{5349 - 4817} · 100 \\[1ex]
&= \frac{301}{532} \cdot 100 \\[1ex]
&= 0.566 \cdot 100 \\[1ex]
pct &= 56.6(\%) \;\; \checkmark
\end{aligned}
$$

 — and now apply $56.6$% to the second range $(6233 ··\;7165)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x & = \left[\dfrac{56.6}{100} \cdot (7165 - 6233)\right] + 6233 \\[1.5ex]
& = [0.566 \cdot 932] + 6233 \\[1.5ex]
& = 627.512 + 6233 \\[1.5ex]
x & = 6760.512\;\; \checkmark
\end{aligned}
$$

By this method we now know that $6760.516$ is the same percentage of the range $(6233 ··\;7165)$ as $5118$ is of the range $(4817 ··\;5349)$ — $56.6$%.

Conceptually, this process preserves **relative position within a range**, not absolute values.
## Getting Fancy

You can combine the two into a single equation:

$$
	y = \left[\left(\frac{x - min_1}{max_1 - min_1}\right) \cdot (max_2 - min_2)\right] + min_2
$$

… but no sane person would ever do such a thing.
