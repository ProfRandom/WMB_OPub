 # Wimby Special Numeric Nomenclature

This section covers several standard math equations and constants for ease of reference.
## Case Signals

Wimby distinguishes the properties/parameters of Duromons and Mesomons from those of Fusomons and Peromons using the case of the symbol:
- Duromons and Mesomons use lower-case:
	- m, r, ρ, g, vₑ
- Fusomons and Peramons use upper-case:
	- M, T, K, R, L, V

This is so that in equations such as:

$$
P = \sqrt{\frac{\alpha^3}{M + m}}
$$

 — it is clear from typography and context that $M$ is the mass of the star and $m$ is the mass of the planet.

If two or more bodies are referenced, numbered subscripts are employed:
- $M_1, M_2, M_3$
- $m_1, m_2, m_3$
- $R_1, R_2, R_3$
- $r_1, r_2, r_3$
- etc.

## Referents

Because the Sun, Earth, Moon, and Jupiter act as basal standards for describing the properties of other things of the same class the symbols
- $s$ ⟶ *solar* ⟶ Sun
- $t$ ⟶ _terran_ ⟶ Earth
- $l$ ⟶ _lunar_ ⟶ Moon
- $j$ ⟶ _jovian_ ⟶ Jupiter

— are used as _referents_; this avoids the need to constantly write out "solar masses", or "Earth radii"; e.g.
 - The mass of the Moon is $0.0123\,t$ or $1\,l$.
 - The mass of the Sun is $333000\,t$ and $1047\,j$.

These also simplify and standardize mathematical expressions of property assignment and relationship.

### Note — Referents in Prose vs Notation

When speaking generally or narratively, Wimby **prefers words** over bare referent symbols.  Use:
- _solar_    
- _terran_    
- _lunar_    
- _jovian_    

(all lower-case)

instead of the raw glyphs when you are **not** giving a strict numerical expression.

For example:
- **Preferred (prose):**  
    “Mars’ mass is a significant fraction of a _terran_ mass.”    
- **Avoid (prose):**  
    “Mars’ mass is a significant fraction of a $t$ mass.”    

## Combined Usages

When necessary, a property symbol may be combined with a referent to ensure clarity of meaning:
- "The resultant value is $33\,m_t$" is acceptable, since "$33_t$" does not inherently specify "mass", and could mean "radius", "gravity", or another property without specification.
### 1. Property Assignment

$$
\begin{array}{c c c}
\text{m} & = & 0.0123\,t \\
\underbrace{\text{property/parameter}}_\text{receptant} & \text{relation} & \underbrace{\text{scalar + referent}}_\text{assignant} \\
\end{array}
$$

- Receptant ⟶ that which is receiving the action of the relation
	- In this case, the symbol of the property/parameter
- Assignant ⟶ that which is assigning the action of the relation
	- In this case, the absolute value being related and the referent element donating the value

### 2. Property Relationship

$$
\begin{array}{c c c}
1\,j & = & 317.8\,t \\
\underbrace{\text{scalar + referent}}_\text{receptant} & \text{relation} & \underbrace{\text{scalar + referent}}_\text{assignant} & 
\end{array}
$$

- Receptant ⟶ that which is receiving the action of the relation
	- In this case, the relative value being related and the symbol of the element receiving the value
- Assignant ⟶ that which is assigning the action of the relation
	- In this case, the absolute value being related and the referent element donating the value

## Denominatorial Radicals
Wimby is fully aware that formal mathematics prefers to **rationalize denominators**, e.g.:

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}} \longrightarrow \dfrac{\sqrt{\rho}}{\rho} \\[0.5ex]
r &=\dfrac{v_e}{\sqrt{\rho}} \longrightarrow \dfrac{v_e\,\sqrt{\rho}}{\rho}
\end{aligned}
$$

However, WMB’s priority is **conceptual transparency**, not textbook orthodoxy.  
In many contexts — especially when illustrating how radius, density, gravity, or escape velocity scale together — it is far clearer to **leave radicals in the denominator**.

Doing so:
- preserves the visible structure of the relationship,    
- reduces algebraic clutter, and    
- keeps the expression aligned with its physical intuition.    

Therefore, WMB does **not** require rationalizing denominators unless it genuinely improves clarity — the resultant value is the same, regardless.

## Fractional Exponents and Indices
Fractional exponents are mathematically valid and compact — for example: $g={(m\,\rho^2})^{1/3}$.

However, Wimby prioritizes **structural readability** over brevity.  To make the hierarchy of operations immediately clear, WMB favors **explicit radicals with integer indices**, even in inline expressions: $g= \sqrt[3]{m\,\rho^2}$.

Using indexed radicals:
- makes the order of operations visually obvious,    
- separates the “outer” operation from the inner algebra,    
- reduces the chance of misreading nested powers, and    
- helps non-specialists parse equations without recalling exponent rules.    

Fractional exponents may still appear where they genuinely improve clarity, but **indexed radicals are the default** for WMB-style presentation.
