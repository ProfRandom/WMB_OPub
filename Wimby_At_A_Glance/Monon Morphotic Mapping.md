# Monon Morphotic Mapping

_When Morphology Becomes Structure_

**Monon Morphotic Mapping** describes **where and when morphotypes become structurally meaningful** across the mass–complexity continuum of monons.

It does **not** define morphotypes — those are specified in **[[Duromon Morphotics]]**.  
It does **not** classify sub-threshold composition — that is covered in **[[Micromon Compositional Regimes]]**.

Its purpose is to identify the **emergence thresholds** at which gravity enforces:

- differentiation    
- phase stability    
- internal layering    
- long-term structural memory    

In short:

> **This article answers the question:  
> “At what point does ‘what it’s made of’ become ‘what it is’?”**
## Conceptual Overview

Morphotics is not scale-invariant.

At small masses, composition exists but **structure does not persist**.  
At larger masses, gravity becomes the dominant organizing force, and **morphology stabilizes**.

Morphotic expression therefore:

- **emerges gradually**    
- **stabilizes discontinuously**    
- **depends on mass, not intent**    

This article maps that transition.
## Monon Superclasses and Morphotic Relevance

Monons fall into several superclasses based on binding regime and internal physics.  
Morphotics applies differently — or not at all — across these classes.

### **Monon Superclasses**

- **Synomons**  
    Cohesion without gravitation; morphotics does not apply.    
- **Duromons**  
    Gravity-dominated classical matter bodies; **formal morphotics emerges here**.    
- **Transomons**  
    Partially degenerate bodies; classical morphotypes give way to **ulsiform regimes**.    
- **Fusomons**  
    Fusion-powered bodies; morphotics is superseded by stellar physics.    
- **Peromons**  
    Relativistic or collapse-dominated structures; morphology is no longer planet-like.    

> **Formal morphotype expression stabilizes only within the duromonic regime.**
## Duromon Mass Regimes and Morphotic Emergence

Within the duromon superclass, morphotic behavior emerges progressively across mass subclasses.
### Micromons (≈10⁻⁶ – 10⁻⁵ t)

- Gravity insufficient for differentiation    
- Composition is descriptive, not structural    
- No stable morphotypes    
- Pre-morphotic regime    

_(See [[Micromon Compositional Regimes]])_
### Minimons (≈10⁻⁴ t)

- Weak gravitational ordering    
- Early, transient differentiation    
- Morphotic tendencies appear but do not persist    
### Midimons (≈10⁻³ t)

- First reliable internal differentiation    
- Stable lithic crusts may form    
- Rheiform behavior appears in volatile-rich bodies    
- Thin, transient aeriform envelopes possible    

> **Midimons mark the threshold where morphotics first becomes meaningful.**
### Mesamons (≈10⁻² t)

- Fully differentiated interiors    
- Stable lithiform structure    
- Persistent rheiform regimes in icy bodies    
- Early atmospheric retention
### Planemons (≈10⁻¹ – 10¹ t)

- Full morphotic maturity    
- Stable expression of:
       - Lithiform        
    - Rheiform        
    - Aeriform        
    - (contextually) Ulsiform components        
- Long-term tectonics, atmospheres, and internal energetics    

This is the domain of **Duromon Morphotics proper**.
## Morphotype Emergence by Regime

_(Threshold Summary)_

- **Lithiform**
    
    - First meaningful expression: **Midimons**        
    - Fully stable: **Mesamons and Planemons**
        
- **Rheiform**
    
    - Appears with internal melting or cryomelts        
    - Common in **icy Mesamons** and **outer-system Planemons**
        
- **Aeriform**
    
    - Requires sustained atmospheric retention        
    - First persistent expression: **Planemons**
        
- **Ulsiform**
    
    - Requires degenerate or exotic pressure regimes        
    - Appears only in **Transomons, Fusomons, and Peromons**
        

No morphotype appears “all at once.”  
Each requires sufficient gravity to **enforce order over time**.
## Key Principle: Emergence, Not Categorization

Morphotics is **emergent**, not intrinsic.

- Composition exists at all scales    
- Structure appears only when gravity dominates    
- Stability arrives only when time and mass align    

Thus:

- Micromons are **material archives**    
- Midimons are **structural experiments**    
- Planemons are **morphotic identities**
