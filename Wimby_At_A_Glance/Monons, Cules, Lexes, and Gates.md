# The Mononic System  
## Structure, Cohesion, and Assemblies

**Mononics** is the study of self-coherent bodies — moons, planets, mesomons, stellamons, and degenerate remnants that populate the **Macrophysical Regime** of the WMB ontology.

Where the Ontic Regimes describe *how matter coheres* at different scales, Mononics begins the moment a body achieves **macrophysical identity**: the boundary, behavior, or effect that distinguishes it from its surroundings.

Monons are the building blocks of everything worldcrafters care about — and the units from which all larger systems are assembled.

## 1 · What Is a Monon?

A **Monon** is any *self-coherent* body with a distinguishable identity, whether fragile or massive, simple or complex.

A body is a monon if it:
- forms a **distinct boundary**,  
- behaves as a **unified whole**, or  
- exerts a **perceptible effect** distinguishable from its environment.

A snowflake, a droplet, a grain of sand, a boulder, a planemon, and a star all qualify — each at a different scale and via different binding mechanisms — but each achieves *unity*.

Monons divide into two fundamental structural types:

| **Type**    | **Binding**     | **Examples**                     |
| ----------- | --------------- | -------------------------------- |
| **Synomon** | Electrochemical | crystals, droplets, hailstones   |
| **Varymon** | Gravitational   | asteroids, moons, planets, stars |

**Synomons** are held together by molecular and crystalline forces.  
**Varymons** are held together by gravity overpowering electrochemical cohesion.

This distinction determines nearly everything about their behavior.

## 2 · Synomons — Electrochemically Bound Monons

Synomons inhabit the lower end of the Macrophysical Regime.  
Their unity is provided by **electrochemical cohesion**, not gravity.

Examples:
- a snowflake  
- a raindrop  
- a quartz crystal  
- a boulder  
- a gold nugget  
- a chunk of obsidian  

Synomons may be:
- **morphotically simple** (a pure diamond)  
- **morphotically structured** (granite, ice composites)

Synons combine into *synonic assemblies*, which are classified morphotically.

## 3 · Synonic Assemblies — Cules, Lexes, Gates

The suffixes **–cule**, **–lex**, and **–gate** describe how monons assemble into larger units. 
These are **morphotic distinctions**, describing sameness, variety, and context.

### 3.1 Synocule — Unity by Likeness

> A group of synomons sharing the **same morphotic nature**.

Examples:  
- a handful of diamonds  
- a pouch of quartz crystals  
- identically manufactured beads  

### 3.2 Synolex — Unity by Variation

> Synomons of *different morphotic expressions* of the same nature.

Example:  
Rubies + sapphires + emeralds + spinels → all minerals, different expressions.

### 3.3 Synogate — Unity by Context

> Synomons of **different morphotic natures** gathered as a contextual whole.

Examples:  
- a scoop of mixed gems + amber + obsidian + bone  
- beach sand (shells, minerals, silica fragments, organics)  
- a mixed rubble pile  

A synogate can be extremely complex — but still not self-gravitating.

## 4 · When Synogates Become Varymons — The Collapse Threshold

A synogate becomes a **varymon** when its mass is large enough that **self-gravity exceeds electrochemical cohesion**.

This generally occurs around:
-  **≈ 10⁻⁶ terran masses — the microterran threshold**

Below this:  
	**EC forces dominate → synonic structure.**

Above this:  
	**Gravity dominates → the body becomes a single varymonic monon**, regardless of heterogeneous composition.

This is how rubble piles become asteroids, icy conglomerates become moons, and protoplanets emerge from dust.
## 5 · Varymons — Gravibound Monons

A **Varymon** is any monon whose integrity comes from gravity.  
They dominate the astrophysical landscape:
- asteroids  
- dwarf planets  
- moons  
- terrestrial planets  
- gas/ice giants  
- mesomons (brown dwarfs)  
- stellamons (stars)  
- fusomons (fusion-powered stars)  
- peromons (collapsed objects)  

Varymons may be:

- **morphotically simple** (a carbon planet)  
- **morphotically complex** (layered worlds, differentiated bodies)

Gravitation becomes the master binder.

## 6 · Varymonic Assemblies — Varycule, Varylex, Varygate

As with synonic assemblies, the suffixes describe *structural organization*, not scale.
### 6.1 Varycule — Unity by Character

> Varymons of the **same morphotic character**.

Example:  
A cluster of S-type asteroids.

### 6.2 Varylex — Unity by Variety

> Varymons of *different morphotic varieties* within the same character.

Example:  
S-type + C-type asteroids in the same family.

### 6.3 Varygate — Unity by Context

> A gravitationally contextual collection of varymons of **different identities and natures**.

Examples:
- a planetary system  
- a binary star with planets  
- a debris field  
- an open cluster  
- a stellar nursery  
- a galaxy  
- a galaxy cluster  

**A varygate can be small or cosmic; the suffix defines structure, not size.**

## 7 · Summary Table — The Mononic Assembly Ladder

| **Level** | **Synonic Track** | **Varymonic Track** | **Binding Principle** |
| --------- | ----------------- | ------------------- | --------------------- |
| **Monon** | Synomon           | Varymon             | EC → Gravity          |
| **Cule**  | Synocule          | Varycule            | sameness              |
| **Lex**   | Synolex           | Varylex             | structured difference |
| **Gate**  | Synogate          | Varygate            | contextual coherence  |

A synogate transitions to a varymon when the gravitational threshold is crossed.  
Gates are scale-neutral — a puddle is a synogate; a galaxy is a varygate.

## 8 · Conceptual Arc

- **Synomons** → coherence through matter  
- **Varymons** → coherence through mass  
- **Cules** → unity by likeness  
- **Lexes** → unity by relationship  
- **Gates** → unity by context  

These structures form the backbone of Mononics, the physical ontology from which all crafted worlds emerge.
