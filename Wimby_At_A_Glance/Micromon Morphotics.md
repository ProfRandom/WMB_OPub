# Micromon Compositional Regimes

_Pre-Morphotic Structure in Sub-Planemon Bodies_

Micromons (Small Stellar System Bodies; **S³B**) are **sub-planemon mass objects** whose internal gravity is insufficient to enforce stable differentiation, long-term phase separation, or persistent morphotic expression.

They include:

- asteroids    
- comets    
- Trojans    
- Kuiper Belt Objects (KBOs)    
- Trans-Neptunian Objects (TNOs)    
- other minor bodies    

Micromons are **ontically distinct monons**, but they occupy a **pre-morphotic regime**: composition exists, but **systemic morphotypes do not yet reliably emerge**.

This article describes **compositional and structural regimes** observable at micromon scale. It does **not** redefine morphotypes, which are treated formally in **[[Duromon Morphotics]]**, nor does it map where morphotypes become stable, which is covered in **[[Monon Morphotic Mapping (older)]]**.
## Scope and Terminology Note (Important)

At micromon scales:

- gravity is weak    
- differentiation is incomplete or transient    
- internal energetics are minimal    
- structural memory is short-lived    

As a result:

> **Morphotic language applies descriptively, not systemically.**

The suffix **–oid** is used throughout this article to indicate **material resemblance**, not morphotic equivalence.

- **Lithoid** ≠ Lithiform    
- **Rheoid** ≠ Rheiform    
- **Ulsoid** ≠ Ulsiform    

Stable morphotypes require duromonic mass regimes.
## Micromon Mass and Size Envelope

| **Micromon Mass Range** | **Upper Bound (µt)** | **Upper Bound (t)** | **Approx. Radius Limit** |
| ----------------------- | -------------------- | ------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Minimum                 | Meteoroid scale      | ≥ 0.0000001 t       | ≥ 1 km                   |
| Maximum                 | 250 µt               | 0.00025 t           | ~600 km                  |
### Boundary Justification

- **Upper mass bound:**  
    Set at **≤ 250 µt (0.00025 t)**, approximately the smallest bodies capable of sustained internal differentiation (e.g., Charon-class objects).    
- **Upper radius bound:**  
    ~600 km, following Tim deBenedictis’ planetary-class threshold.    
- **Bodies above this regime** transition toward **midimon** behavior and emergent morphotics.    
- **Meteoroids** remain a **size-based category**, not a morphotic or compositional class.   
## Micromon Compositional Classes

_(Pre-Somatypic Descriptors)_

These categories describe **dominant material makeup and inferred origin**, not stable morphotypes or somatypes.
### Lithoid Micromons

_Rock- and metal-dominated_

| Subtype               | Composition                     | Examples         | Notes                                     |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------- | ---------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| **S-Type Telluroid**  | Silicate-rich                   | Eros, Vesta      | High albedo, stony                        |
| **C-Type Telluroid**  | Carbonaceous, hydrated minerals | Pallas, Mathilde | Dark, primitive                           |
| **Vestoid Telluroid** | Pyroxene-rich                   | Vesta, Vestoids  | Likely fragments of differentiated bodies |
| **M-Type Telluroid**  | Metallic                        | Psyche           | Possible planetesimal cores               |

Lithoids are **compositionally analogous** to lithiform bodies, but lack the mass required for tectonics, sustained mantles, or long-term internal layering.
### Rheoid Micromons

_Volatile-dominated_

| Subtype            | Composition                    | Examples        | Notes                                            |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------ | --------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **Pagoid Rheoid**  | Frozen volatiles               | 67P, Halley     | Standard icy bodies                              |
| **Fluxoid Rheoid** | Liquid-dominated               | (hypothetical)  | Possible transient surface liquids               |
| **Ceroid Rheoid**  | Ice + subsurface liquid layers | Ceres, Callisto | Transitional bodies approaching midimon behavior |

Rheoids may exhibit **episodic melting or cryovolcanism**, but cannot sustain stable rheiform morphologies without greater mass.
### Aeriform Micromons

_(Rare / Transient)_

True aeriform regimes require atmospheric mass dominance and sustained retention.  
At micromon scale, aeriform behavior is **temporary and non-self-maintaining**, typically resulting from outgassing or recent impact events.

As such, aeriform micromons are **phenomenological states**, not stable classes.
### Ulsoid Micromons

_Exotic or cryptic composition_

| Subtype             | Composition                | Examples      | Notes                               |
| ------------------- | -------------------------- | ------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| **Exotoid Ulsoid**  | Hypothetical exotic matter | (speculative) | Strangelets, dark-matter inclusions |
| **Cryptoid Ulsoid** | Unknown or anomalous       | ‘Oumuamua     | Unclassified origin or structure    |

Ulsoids represent **observational outliers**, not a coherent morphotic regime. Their inclusion preserves taxonomic openness without asserting premature explanations.
## Structural Considerations

At micromon scales:

- internal pressure is low    
- gravity does not dominate cohesion    
- differentiation is partial or absent    
- resurfacing is episodic and often catastrophic    

As a result:

- **composition ≠ structure**    
- **material ≠ morphology**    
- **origin ≠ destiny**    

Micromons are **material archives**, not systems.
## Relation to Morphotics Proper

- **Morphotics** becomes meaningful once gravity enforces internal order.    
- This begins weakly at **midimon** scales and stabilizes at **planemon** scales.    
- Micromons therefore occupy the **pre-morphotic regime**.

## Final Notes

This classification:

- preserves observational rigor    
- avoids teleology    
- prevents premature morphotic claims    
- remains extensible as new micromon classes are discovered    

Micromons are not failed planets.

They are the **raw material from which worlds eventually condense**.
