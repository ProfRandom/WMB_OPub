# Relating Animotics to Planemonics

## _Structural Eligibility vs. Physical Constraint_

### Why This Article Exists

**Animotics** and **Planemonics** answer different questions.

- **Animotics** asks:
    
    > _What kinds of animosys are physically possible?_
    
- **Planemonics** asks:
    
    > _What kinds of physical bodies exist, and under what constraints?_
    

Worldbuilders, however, inevitably ask a third question:

> _If I want a particular kind of planetary environment or animosys, what kind of world do I need to build?_

This article exists to answer that question **without collapsing the distinction between ontology and design**.

It provides **correlations, not definitions**.  
It describes **constraints, not guarantees**.  
It is **prescriptive in use**, but **descriptive in theory**.

## Directionality (Explicit and Non-Negotiable)

The relationship between Animotics and Planemonics is **one-way**.

> **Animotics defines what kind of animosys is possible.**  
> **Planemonics constrains which physical bodies can host it.**

The arrow **never points in reverse**.

- A planet’s mass, gravity, or radius **does not define** its animotic class.    
- An animotic class **does not imply** a specific planet type.    

However:

- Certain planemonic regimes **strongly correlate** with certain animotic states.    
- Other regimes **exclude them outright**.    

This article documents those correlations for **design guidance**, not classification.
## The Core Principle: Eligibility vs. Realization

Animotics describes **structural eligibility**.

Whether animosys actually appears — and how it develops — depends on **milieutics**:

- formation history    
- volatile delivery    
- stellar evolution    
- catastrophic resets    
- path dependence    

Thus:

> **Animotics answers “could.”  
> Milieutics answers “did.”**

Planemonics constrains the “could” space; it never answers the “did.”
## Typical Planemonic Correlates by Animotic Class

The following sections describe describe a **high-probability overlap phase space**, not exclusive requirements.
### Biotic Order (COH-amegic)

**Animotic definition:**  
COH-animosys is physically possible.

**Typical planemonic correlates:**

- Duromons (solid-surface bodies)    
- Mass sufficient for long-term gravity retention    
- Stable pressure–temperature regimes permitting liquid-phase solvents    
- Silicate–metal compositions    
- Atmospheres capable of cycling volatiles    

**Exclusions:**

- Airless micromons    
- Degenerate mesomons    
- Fusomons and peromons    
- Purely cryogenic or plasma-dominated bodies   

> A world may be Earth-sized and still _not_ be Biotic if its chemistry excludes COH viability.
#### High-Probability Biotic Planemonic Phase Space

The following parameter ranges describe a **typical phase space** in which **Biotic (COH-amegic) animosys is most likely to be structurally supportable**.

These values:

- are **empirical correlations**, not definitions    
- describe **high-probability overlap**, not hard boundaries    
- do **not** imply animosys will emerge    
- do **not** exclude Biotic worlds outside these ranges    

They exist solely as **design guidance**.

$$
\text{BIOTIC PLANEMONIC PHASE SPACE} := \left\{
\begin{aligned}
	&0.05 ≤ m ≤ 3 \\
	&0.35 ≤ \rho ≤ 2.18 \\
	&0.35 ≤ r(m, \rho) ≤ 2.30 \\
	&0.25 ≤ g(m, r) ≤ 3.00 \\
	&0.30 ≤ v_e(g, r) ≤ 2.63
\end{aligned}
\right.
$$

### Terric Domain (mesanthropic Biotic)

**Animotic definition:**  
COH-animosys is possible within a human-adjacent physical envelope.

**Typical planemonic correlates:**

- Planemons in the mid-mass duromon range    
- Surface gravities broadly compatible with human physiology    
- Atmospheric retention sufficient for weathering and hydrospheres    
- Internal heat budgets capable of tectonic cycling    

Terrics are the **broadest physically meaningful Biotic envelope** and allow substantial diversity in:

- climate    
- atmospheric composition    
- surface habitability

- do **not** imply animosys will emerge

### Geotic Realm (maxanthropic Biotic)

**Animotic definition:**  
Stable, moderate, Earthlike environments capable of supporting long-term open-air COH-animosys.

**Typical planemonic correlates:**

- Narrower mass and density clustering than Terrics    
- Balanced gravity for both biomechanics and atmospheric stability    
- Escape velocities tuned for climate moderation    
- Long-lived tectonic and magnetic activity    

Geotics occupy the **structural core** of the Terric Domain.

They are not Earth clones — they are **Earth-mode worlds**.

### Gaean Province (melanthropic Biotic)

**Animotic definition:**  
The deep anthropic sweet spot: worlds that are freely habitable by humans without adaptation.

**Typical planemonic correlates:**

- Tight clustering around Earth-normal gravity    
- Narrow radius and density ranges    
- Exceptionally stable climates    
- Robust biosphere–atmosphere feedback loops    

> Gaeans are rare not because they are complex, but because they are **narrow**.

Every Gaean is Biotic, Terric, and Geotic — but only a small fraction of any of those classes are Gaean.

### Kybelic Domain (melamegic Biotic)

COH-worlds optimized for biospheric productivity rather than human comfort.

**Typical planemonic correlates:**

- High-mass duromons    
- Elevated surface gravity    
- Dense atmospheres    
- Strong internal heat and nutrient cycling    

Kybelics are:

- morphotically Terric    
- animotically lateral    
- anthropically hostile    

They are **superhabitable for COH-animosys**, not for humans.

### Xenotic Order (non-COH animosys)

**Animotic definition:**  
Animosys based on non-COH chemistry.

**Planemonic correlates:**

- Extremely broad    
- May include:    
    - icy worlds        
    - gas giants        
    - high-pressure interiors        
    - exotic crusts        
    - deep atmospheres        

**Key point:**

> A world may fall entirely within Terric, Geotic, or even Gaean _parameters_ and still be Xenotic if its animochemistry is alien to COH assumptions.

Physical similarity does not imply biochemical familiarity.

### Astatic Order (field-based animosys)

**Animotic definition:**  
Animosys arising from plasma, magnetic, gravitational, or quantum field coherence.

**Planemonic relevance:**  
None.

Astatics are **not planemon-hosted**.

They may:

- inhabit stellar magnetospheres    
- emerge in plasma flows    
- stabilize in gravitational saddlepoints    
- persist as non-localized field structures    

Planemonics does not constrain Astatics, because **they do not require bodies**.
#### **⟩⟩ Why There Is No Astatic Planemonic Phase Space**

Unlike Biotic, Terric, Geotic, Gaean, Kybelic, or Xenotic animosys, the **Astatic Order does not admit a planemonic phase space**.  This is not an omission.   It is a structural consequence of what Astatics are.

Astatic animosys arises from **field coherence**, not from **planemon-hosted environments**. These systems may emerge within:

- plasma flows    
- magnetic or electric field architectures    
- gravitational saddlepoints    
- relativistic regimes    
- quantum-coherent structures    

They do **not** require:

- a solid surface   
- a bounded planetary body    
- a stable mass–radius relationship    
- gravitational self-coherence at planemon scale    

As a result, traditional planemonic parameters — mass, radius, surface gravity, escape velocity, bulk density — are **not primary constraints** for Astatic animosys.

There is therefore no meaningful way to define a “high-probability planemonic phase space” for Astatics without committing a category error.

> **Astatics are constrained by field topology, flux stability, and coherence duration — not by planetary envelopes.**

Design guidance for Astatic animosys belongs to:

- plasma physics    
- magnetohydrodynamics    
- gravitational dynamics    
- quantum field theory    

—not to Planemonics.
## Design Guidance (Explicitly Non-Ontological)

This article exists so a worldbuilder can say:

- _“I want a Gaean-like environment — what should I aim for physically?”_    
- _“I want COH-animosys but not human habitability.”_    
- _“I want a familiar-looking world with alien animosys.”_    
- _“I want maximal biospheric productivity at the expense of comfort.”_    

Animotics tells you **what kind of animosys you are targeting**.  
Planemonics tells you **what physical envelopes make that plausible**.

Neither replaces the other.
## What This Article Does _Not_ Claim

- It does **not** guarantee animosys.    
- It does **not** collapse classification into parameters.    
- It does **not** imply moral or narrative outcomes.    
- It does **not** replace milieutic history.    

> **Correlation is not destiny.  
> Structure is not outcome.**

## Summary

- Animotics and Planemonics answer different questions.    
- Their relationship is **correlative, not definitional**.    
- The arrow of implication runs **Animotics → Planemonics**, never backward.    
- This article exists to support **design**, not ontology.    
- Worlds are eligible; histories decide.    

Wimby does not tell you what _will_ happen on a world.

It tells you what the universe would permit — and leaves the rest to history, chance, and choice.
