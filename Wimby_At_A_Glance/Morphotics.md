# Morphotics

**Morphotics** describes the **dominant matter regime** of a monon — what kind of material structure it expresses once coherence is achieved.

It applies to **all monons**, from synomons to peromons, but its expressions vary by mass regime.

- Field: **Morphotics**    
- Scope: **material composition, phase behavior, internal structure**    
- Independence: **orthogonal to animosys**    

> **Morphotics answers:**  
> _What is this body made of, structurally speaking?_

It does **not** answer:

- whether life exists    
- whether life is possible    
- whether a world is habitable    

Those are animotic overlays.
### Core Axes Summary

- **Mononic Axis** — _how_ a body coheres (electrochemical vs gravitational)    
- **Morphotic Axis** — _what kind of matter regime_ that coherence expresses    
- **Categorical Envelope** — how those traits behave within a system    

> Morphotics qualifies **substance**, not **outcome**.
## Morphotype

A **Morphotype** is the primary compositional and structural descriptor of a monon — its dominant material regime.

Canonical morphotypes include:

- **Lithiform** — solid rock–metal bodies    
- **Rheiform** — partially molten or cryo-fluid bodies    
- **Aeriform** — bodies dominated by gaseous or fluid envelopes    
- **Ulsiform** — degenerate or exotic matter regimes    

Morphotypes are **ontic**, not ecological.
## Monon Morphotic Mapping

This article maps **where morphotypes stabilize** across the mononic mass continuum.  
It does **not** define the morphotypes themselves.

Full morphotype definitions live in **[[Duromon Morphotics]]**.
### When Morphotics Becomes Meaningful

Morphotic expression requires:

- sufficient mass for differentiation    
- sustained internal energetics    
- stable phase domains    

This begins weakly in **midimons**, stabilizes in **mesamons**, and reaches full expression in **planemons**.
## Monon Superclasses (Context)

- **Synomons** — electrochemically bound; morphotics trivial or local    
- **Duromons** — gravitationally coherent classical matter bodies    
- **Transomons** — partially degenerate    
- **Fusomons** — fusion-dominated    
- **Peromons** — relativistic or collapse-dominated    

Classical morphotypes dominate **duromons**.  
**Ulsiform morphotics** dominates beyond them.
## Duromon Mass Subclasses

|Class|Approx. Mass|Morphotic Expression|
|---|---|---|
|Micromons|10⁻⁶–10⁻⁵ t|No stable differentiation|
|Minimons|~10⁻⁴ t|Weak, transient morphotics|
|Midimons|~10⁻³ t|First stable lithiformity|
|Mesamons|~10⁻² t|Full internal differentiation|
|Planemons|10⁻¹–10¹ t|Complete morphotic maturity|

Morphotics **matures with mass**, not time.
## Where Each Morphotype Appears

### Lithiform

- Requires mineral differentiation and pressure    
- First stabilizes in **midimons**    
- Fully expressed in **mesamons and planemons**    
### Rheiform

- Partial melt or cryo-fluid regimes    
- Common in volatile-rich mesamons and outer-system planemons    
- Often subsurface
### Aeriform

- Requires atmospheric retention    
- Appears at mesamon scale    
- Dominant in volatile-rich planemons    
### Ulsiform

- Requires degenerate or exotic pressure regimes    
- Appears only in **transomons, fusomons, and peromons**    
## Morphotypes vs Animotics

Morphotypes are **structural facts**.

- A lithiform world may be sterile    
- A rheiform world may host subsurface animosys    
- An aeriform world may support exotic ecosystems    
- An ulsiform body may host **Astatic animosys**    

> **Morphotics constrains animotics.  
> It never defines it.**
## Transition Rule (Critical)

A synonic assembly becomes a varymon when **self-gravity overwhelms electrochemical cohesion**.

At that moment:

- Morphotics transitions from local to global    
- Differentiation becomes inevitable    
- The body acquires a persistent material identity    

This is the ontic gateway to planetary structure.
